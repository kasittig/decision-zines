# FIRE, SAFETY & SYSTEMS

## INTENT

Your organization knows how to manage a planned emergency. What do you
do when substantially the same hazard happens without being planned?

Fire itself was not anomalous at the event. The event intentionally
supported art designed to burn and had a system for managing it. Before
the event the incident year, official participant communications required advance
registration and Fire Core approval for art intended to burn so that
fire-safety planning could occur. The sculpture did not have a burn plan.
When it burned on Burn Night, Safety was therefore responding not simply
to fire, but to an unplanned version of a hazard the organization
already knew how to manage when planned.

The second problem develops after the immediate incident: what does
Safety do when organizational leadership may not agree with Safety about
how the incident should be followed up or understood? The historical
record establishes disagreement and concern; it does not establish that
Organizational Leadership obstructed Safety or that Safety actually stifled community
discussion.

## WHO ARE YOU?

You are part of the event's Safety leadership, responding first to an
unplanned fire and then to the organizational follow-up.

## ROLES IN THIS STORY

Safety — investigates hazards, system vulnerabilities, and future
prevention.

Fire — communicates and manages fire-safety expectations.

Organizational Leadership — handles governance, attribution, and
discipline.

Participants / Witnesses — may provide direct observations or other
information.

## TIMELINE

- **Burn Night:** An art object burns without an approved burn plan.
- **After the Event:** Safety seeks information and reviews the incident.
- **Following Months:** Safety and organizational leadership separate operational learning from attribution and discipline.
- **Investigation Closure:** Attribution remains unresolved in the surviving record.

## DECISION 1 — SOMETHING UNEXPECTED IS BURNING

## CONTEXT

A large art object is burning at an event where intentional burns are
normal.

## WHAT DO YOU DO?

A. It is Burn Night; treat it like the other fires.

B. Treat it as an unplanned fire incident and determine what controls
are missing.

C. First determine who the artist is and whether they wanted it burned.

D. Something else.

## DECISION BOUNDARY

## WHAT THE RECORD SHOWS

The sculpture burned at approximately 11 p.m. on Burn Night. Ranger
and Fire Safety leadership later stated that the artist did not have a
burn plan and that the organization did not know who ignited it. They
said the burn violated established fire-art rules and did not condone
it.

## DECISION 2 — THE FIRE IS OUT. IS THE INCIDENT OVER?

## CONTEXT

The immediate fire has ended. There may have been no catastrophic
outcome. You still do not know why the burn occurred or who ignited the
object.

## WHAT DO YOU DO?

A. Close the incident because the immediate hazard has passed.

B. Conduct an After Action Review focused on system vulnerabilities and
future prevention.

C. Focus primarily on identifying and disciplining the person who
ignited it.

D. Hand the entire matter to Organizational Leadership.

E. Something else.

## DECISION BOUNDARY

## WHAT THE RECORD SHOWS

Safety framed its follow-up through the After Action Report process. Its
public request said the purpose was to identify vulnerabilities in
systems and improve future operations, and asked direct witnesses with
relevant information to contact Fire or submit an AAR. Safety also
stated that its goal was not to suppress experimental or risky art, but
to support it through reasonable risk mitigation.

## DECISION 3 — WHAT EXACTLY ARE YOU INVESTIGATING?

## CONTEXT

Witness information is incomplete. People want to know who did it.
Safety also needs to understand how an unplanned burn became possible.

## WHAT DO YOU DO?

Which questions belong to the safety investigation?

Questions may include:

- Why was an unplanned burn possible?
- Which safeguards or planning processes were bypassed?
- What would reduce the chance or consequences of recurrence?
- What did witnesses directly observe?
- Who physically ignited the object?
- Who should determine individual responsibility or discipline?

## DECISION BOUNDARY

## WHAT THE RECORD SHOWS

By January the following year, the event explicitly separated functions. Fire was tasked
with communicating the general lesson that unplanned burns were
unacceptable, while Organizational Leadership stated that it—not Safety—would pursue
the question of the person responsible. Safety could provide information
from its investigation.

## DECISION 4 — WHAT IF SAFETY AND ORGANIZATIONAL LEADERSHIP DISAGREE?

## CONTEXT

Organizational leadership later expresses concern that the way Safety
gathered information may have affected a broader community or artistic
conversation. Safety still has a legitimate need to understand an
unauthorized fire.

## WHAT DO YOU DO?

A. Stop the investigation because the Organizational Leadership objects to its effects.

B. Continue exactly as before because Safety owns all questions
involving the incident.

C. Clarify Safety's operational purpose, document what is known, and
separate system-learning work from governance, discipline, and broader
cultural discussion.

D. Escalate the disagreement as a governance question while preserving
the operational record.

E. Something else.

## DECISION BOUNDARY

## WHAT THE RECORD SHOWS

August the incident year Board minutes record concern that Safety's
information-gathering tone might have changed subsequent online
discussion and may have short-circuited broader discussion hoped for by
the artist. The minutes establish that Organizational Leadership held this concern. They
do not establish that Safety actually suppressed or changed the
conversation, and the underlying online discussion is presently
unavailable for independent review.

## DECISION 5 — DO YOU NEED TO SOLVE THE MYSTERY TO FIX THE SAFETY PROBLEM?

## CONTEXT

The attribution investigation continues for months. Evidence remains
incomplete and the identity of the person who physically ignited the
sculpture is not definitively established in the surviving record.

## WHAT DO YOU DO?

Can Safety still close the operational loop?

A. Yes.

B. No.

## DECISION BOUNDARY

## WHAT THE RECORD SHOWS

The organization eventually separated the general safety message from
sculpture-specific attribution. The organizational record says a general
unplanned-burn statement was completed and reviewed, although no
corresponding official Announce publication has been located. The
attribution and disciplinary process continued separately and ultimately
ended without a definitive surviving factual finding identifying the
igniter.

## LOOK BACK AT YOUR DECISIONS

Safety never needs the sculpture to become a solved whodunit in order to
learn from it.

The operational questions are simpler and more durable:

- What happened outside the plan?
- Why was it possible?
- What controls were absent or bypassed?
- What does the organization need to know next time?
- Which questions belong to Safety, and which need to move somewhere else?
